# State Changes, Migration, and Expiry

Mindfish is dynamic. A hypothesis is valid only within a defined topic and observation window. It should not remain current merely because it was useful in the past.

The terms below are descriptive lifecycle verbs, not a validated universal stage model:

- **EMERGING** — a new, weakly observed tension or question appears.
- **GATHERING** — multiple observations suggest the same tension is becoming more visible.
- **MIGRATING** — attention or expression appears to move to another context, platform, actor, or framing.
- **SPLITTING** — one broad hypothesis contains distinct tensions that need separate treatment.
- **FADING** — supporting observations become less frequent or relevant in the current window.
- **RECURRING** — a similar tension appears again after a gap; recurrence does not prove continuity or common cause.
- **EXPIRED** — the time window or review condition has passed; the record remains historical, not current.

## Safeguards

1. Do not infer a transition from one isolated observation.
2. Require timestamps and comparable observation definitions.
3. Keep source coverage and missingness visible.
4. Do not merge topics solely because wording is similar.
5. Do not infer causality from temporal sequence or co-occurrence.
6. Allow a hypothesis to split, be contested, or return to UNKNOWN.
7. Make expiry explicit.

Hysteresis and transition thresholds remain open design questions. Any threshold must be preregistered and evaluated out of time, not repeatedly tuned against the same historical window.
