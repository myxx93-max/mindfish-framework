# Observation Loop

## Goal

Use Mindfish to select the next useful observation or verification step, not to produce an impressive taxonomy.

## Loop

1. **Trigger** — a hotspot, event, contradiction, or meaningful change enters scope.
2. **Bound** — define topic identity, observation window, and source scope.
3. **Observe** — collect lawful, attributable, time-stamped evidence.
4. **Hypothesize** — create the smallest useful Mindfish hypothesis, or record `UNKNOWN`.
5. **Route** — choose `OBSERVE`, `SUPPLEMENT`, `VERIFY`, or a bounded `ACT` step.
6. **Countercheck** — seek disconfirming evidence, missing voices, and alternative explanations.
7. **Decide** — keep, split, merge, downgrade, or reject the hypothesis.
8. **Expire** — close the observation window and prevent stale states from appearing current.
9. **Learn** — record whether the chosen direction produced useful new evidence or a better decision.

## Evidence discipline

- Preserve source identity, retrieval time, publication time, and context.
- Separate primary records, participant statements, secondary reporting, and model interpretation.
- Repetition is not independent corroboration.
- Comments are evidence of expression, not automatically evidence that the underlying claim is true.
- Missing data is not zero.
- UNKNOWN is a valid result.
- Confidence scores require a defined calibration method.

Attention Flow may prioritize which topics or windows to inspect. It cannot replace source verification, safety checks, or authorization.

`ACT` means a proposed next step in research or content workflow. It does not authorize publication, outreach, account action, purchase, or other consequential external operation.
